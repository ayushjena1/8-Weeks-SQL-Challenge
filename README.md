## 📖 Project Summary

The **8 Week SQL Challenge** is a set of real-world business case studies created by [Danny Ma](https://www.datawithdanny.com). Each one drops you into a different company, from a small restaurant to a digital bank to a marketing agency, and asks you to answer the questions its team actually needs answered.

This repository contains **my complete solutions to all 8 case studies**, written in **PostgreSQL**. Across the project I:

- 🧹 **Cleaned messy data**: `'null'` strings, unit text inside numeric columns, comma-separated lists and text-formatted dates.
- 🔁 **Built reusable objects**: views, cleaned tables and a new ratings table with constraints.
- 📊 **Answered business questions** on customers, revenue, churn, funnels, campaigns and product performance.
- 🪟 **Used advanced SQL**: CTEs, window functions, percentiles, rolling averages, recursive-style hierarchy joins and self-joins.
- 🧠 **Explained my reasoning**: each folder has its own README covering the business problem, my approach to every question, and the key learnings.

> 💼 **Why it matters:** every case study mirrors a real analytics task (churn analysis, funnel conversion, before/after impact, customer segmentation), which is exactly the kind of work SQL is used for day to day.

**🎯 Business problem:** Danny's new Japanese restaurant (sushi, curry, ramen) has collected basic data but doesn't know how to use it. He wants to understand **visiting patterns, spending and favourite dishes** to decide whether to expand his **loyalty program**.

**🔍 What I did:** answered all 10 questions plus 2 bonus tasks: first and most-popular items, before/after-membership behaviour, and a **loyalty points system** with a first-week 2× bonus.

### [2️⃣ Pizza Runner](./pizza_runner/)

**🎯 Business problem:** Danny is "Uberising" his pizza business with runners and a mobile app. The raw order and delivery data is messy and needs **cleaning** before he can measure **runner performance, popular toppings and profitability**.

**🔍 What I did:** cleaned NULLs, units and data types; built two helper views to split comma-separated toppings; answered 28 questions across pizza metrics, runner experience, ingredient optimisation and pricing; designed a **new ratings table** with a `CHECK` constraint; and showed how the schema handles a new menu item.

**🛠️ Skills:** data cleaning · `REGEXP_REPLACE` · `UNNEST` / `LATERAL` · views · `STRING_AGG` · `CREATE TABLE` / `INSERT`

### [4️⃣ Data Bank](./data_bank/)

**🎯 Business problem:** Data Bank is a digital-only bank that pairs banking with secure cloud data storage tied to account balances. The team needs to understand **how customers are distributed across nodes** and **how their balances and transactions behave**.

**🔍 What I did:** explored node and region allocation, calculated reallocation times with **median, 80th and 95th percentiles**, computed **month-end closing balances** per customer, and measured the share of customers whose balance grew by more than 5%.

**🛠️ Skills:** `PERCENTILE_CONT` · running totals · `FIRST_VALUE` / `LAST_VALUE` · `DATE_TRUNC` · conditional aggregation

### [5️⃣ Data Mart](./data_mart/)

**🎯 Business problem:** In June 2020 Data Mart switched all products to **sustainable packaging**. Danny needs to know **what happened to sales**, which parts of the business were affected most, and how to plan future changes.

**🔍 What I did:** built a clean analysis table in a **single query**, explored sales by platform, region and demographic, ran a **before-and-after analysis** around 2020-06-15 (4-week and 12-week windows, compared with 2018 and 2019), and ranked the impact across five business dimensions.

**🛠️ Skills:** `CREATE TABLE AS` · date parsing · `EXTRACT` · window functions · before/after analysis

### [8️⃣ Fresh Segments](./fresh_segments/)

**🎯 Business problem:** Fresh Segments is a digital marketing agency that analyses online ad click behaviour. Danny wants **high-level insights** from one major client's interest data: what is stable, what is volatile, and what to recommend.

**🔍 What I did:** cleaned and validated the data (date conversion, NULL handling, join checks), filtered out interests present for fewer than 6 months, found the **top, bottom and most volatile interests**, and calculated **average composition** and a **3-month rolling average** from the index value, with written business recommendations.

## ▶️ How to Run This Project

1. **Install PostgreSQL** and connect with your favourite client (I used **VS Code + SQLTools**).
2. **Pick a case study folder** and run its `Dataset.sql` to create the schema, tables and data.
3. **Run the query files in order** (for example A → B → C → D). Each case study's first script sets the `search_path` to its schema.
4. Where a case study has a cleaning step (**Pizza Runner** and **Data Mart**), run that file **before** the analysis files, because later scripts depend on the cleaned views and tables.

> 📌 Each folder's README has case-specific run notes.
